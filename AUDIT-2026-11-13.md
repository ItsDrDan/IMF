# Sticky Molecules: audit and v16 (2026-11-13)

**Status:** v16 was pushed and is live as `index.html` (2026-11-13). It was built as the working file `sticky-molecules-v16.html`. The comparisons below are against v15.

## 1. Summary

The v15 game was already strong: no wrong answer key was found in any topic, every choice had its own feedback, and the nine simulations were built into practice. The weaknesses were narrower:

- **Answer checking let some wrong methods through** and rejected some reasonable typing.
- **Several hints pointed the wrong way** or did not fit the question they were attached to.
- **One feedback line was false** (HCl: "every H is bonded to carbon").
- **A hint did not count against anything**, so "mastery" could not be told apart from "got it with help", and a missed topic never came back on purpose.
- **On simulation questions the Hint and Show answer buttons sat below the whole simulation.**

v16 fixes those and adds three learning features: hints in steps, topic mastery, and a second look at missed topics.

## 2. How it was audited

- Four independent reviewers each read one quarter of the chemistry code line by line (helpers and tile 1; tiles 2 and 3 and the H-Bond Lab; tiles 4 and 5 and number entry; tiles 6 and 7 and the small labs). They checked values from their own knowledge and ran the generators.
- I read the interface code, wrote two test scripts (now in `tools/`), and played the game in the browser preview as a new student, a struggling student, a strong student and a returning student.

## 4. Answer checking

| Problem in v15 | v16 |
| --- | --- |
| Mole fraction: the wrong method n ÷ n(water) was accepted in 28% of questions. | Never accepted when it differs at 3 sig figs (0 of 3,010 in the test). |
| Raoult's law: typing the pure-water vapor pressure, or X = 1, was accepted in about 7% of questions. | 0 of 2,628. The solutions are also less dilute. |
| "7.84 atm" and "1.2×10³" were rejected with "Type a number". | Units after the number and superscript exponents are read. "1.2x103" is no longer misread as 1200. |
| "12" for 11.5 got "Check your setup". | Gets "rounds correctly, but only 2 sig figs", and does not count as a miss. |

## 5. Learning features added

- **Hints in steps.** The first hint gives the idea. A second hint does part of the work: it rules out one choice and says why, shows the setup with the last number left as "?", or gives the first item in a ranking.
- **Help is recorded.** A correct answer after a hint earns 6 XP (10 without), and the streak neither grows nor resets.
- **Topic mastery.** Three in a row with no hint and no miss masters a topic. Topic buttons show three dots, tiles show "2 of 7 topics mastered", and the feedback says "1 of 3 in a row on your own". It is a count, not an estimate.
- **Second look.** A topic missed in Practice comes back about three questions later, labeled. It is limited to one in three questions and three topics waiting. A second miss suggests the worked example.
- **"See a worked example"** button after a miss, which opens Learn on that topic.
- **New topics in order.** Topics a student has never seen arrive one at a time in each tile's order. A new 3A student now starts with the H-Bond Lab question, not the Energy Well.
- **Hint and Show answer sit under the question**, above the simulation.
- **Accessibility.** Right and wrong choices carry ✓ and ✗, not color alone. The hint box is announced. Number keys 1 to 4 pick a choice.
- **Nine new questions** in the thinnest banks: ion–dipole (2), sugars (2), surface tension and viscosity (2), Energy Well (2), Electron Coin-Flip (1).

## 7. For Dr. Dan to decide

1. **Pentane vapor pressure.** The Raoult items and Solutions Lab use 425 torr at 25 °C. The handbook value at 25 °C is about 512 torr (425 is about 20 °C). Hexane's 151 torr is right for 25 °C. Left as is because it matches a common textbook problem.
2. **The nine new questions** (section 5) are from general knowledge and need your read.
3. **XP for a hinted answer** is now 6, not 10. The style guide says 10 "per independent correct answer", so I read a hinted answer as not independent.
4. **Butane 273 K** replaces 274 K. Change it back if Lecture 28 says 274.
5. Small items left alone: CO₂ critical point 31.1 °C and 73 atm (literature 31.0 and 72.9); chlorine −35 °C (handbook −34); arachidonic acid is on the Sticky Sheet but in no question; mole fraction questions count formula units for ionic solutes.
